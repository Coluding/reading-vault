1Why diffusion is slow — and the one lever everything pulls

Deterministic sampling is just numerical integration of a velocity field along the path from noise to data ([[blogs/dieleman-diffusion-integral]]). Independently-paired noise & data force the field to average over crossing paths → curved trajectories → many ODE steps. Almost every efficiency idea below is an attack on that curve: straighten it, jump across it, reuse work along it, or make each step cheaper.

Spotlight — are consistency models learning the integral?

Yes — and that's exactly why they sample in one step.

A diffusion denoiser only gives the integrand: the instantaneous tangent v(xt, t) at the current point. Sampling re-evaluates it and accumulates many small steps — that's the cost.

x0 = F(xs, s, 0) = xs + ∫s0 v(xτ, τ) dτ

A consistency model is the flow map with its target time pinned to t = 0 (the data end). It maps any point on a trajectory straight to that trajectory's clean endpoint — so what it outputs is the definite integral of the velocity from s all the way to 0. One evaluation replaces the whole integration loop.

Why it's called "consistency": every point on the same ODE path must map to the same x0 — i.e. F(·, s, 0) is invariant as you slide s. That's the compositionality / self-consistency rule with the target fixed at 0, anchored by F(xt, t, t) = xt. Enforcing that invariance is what forces the network to actually be a correct integral.

analyst's view Consistency models are the restricted flow map (one free time, target = 0); CTM lifts them to the full two-time map F(xs,s,t). No free lunch: learning the integral is harder than the integrand — the loss needs stop-gradient, and consistency distillation bootstraps v from a diffusion teacher.

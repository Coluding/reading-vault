## TL;DR

A long (~18k word) manifesto from Packy McCormick, co-written with the team at his World Model startup **General Intuition**, arguing that World Models are a genuinely new class of foundation model — distinct from LLMs and from video models — and the real path to embodied general intelligence. The core technical claim: whereas an LLM predicts the next token $P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)$ and a passive video model predicts the next frame, a World Model predicts the next *state conditioned on an action*, $P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t, a_t)$ — and that action-conditioning is "the magic" that lets a neural net collapse an intractable, stochastic simulation (a stadium of thousands of fans) into a single fixed-cost forward pass ("compute for the uncomputable"). The essay traces the field's eight-year modern history (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018) through four "waves," taxonomizes every major lab's approach (latent/JEPA vs generative/diffusion; VLAs vs World-Model agents), and lands on General Intuition's own bet: that **action-labeled gaming data** (from McCormick's viral clip platform Medal, 1B+ clips/year) is the most elegant answer to the data bottleneck that stalls everyone else. Worth remembering as the single best popular-audience map of the 2026 World Models landscape, and as an explicit statement of the "games-as-ground-truth" thesis.

## Context

Published March 2026 on Not Boring (notboring.co) as an evangelizing "red pill" essay. McCormick is a well-known tech essayist who is also **co-founder of General Intuition** (and of Medal, the gaming-clip platform that supplies GI's training data), so the piece is simultaneously a field survey and a company pitch — it says so openly, quoting NVIDIA's Jim Fan that "very few understand how far-reaching this shift is" and welcoming the reader into "the very few." It responds to a moment of enormous capital flooding into World Models: Yann LeCun's AMI ($1.03B raise, $3.5B val), Fei-Fei Li's World Labs ($1B raise, $5.4B val), Wayve ($1.2B), Physical Intelligence ($600M), plus GI's own $133.7M seed. The essay's implicit debate partner is the "language/code + scale is all you need for AGI" camp; McCormick argues language is "an incredibly lossy compression of reality" and that real intelligence requires embodied contact with the world.

## Core argument

**1. Traditional simulation is computationally intractable; World Models make it a fixed cost.** Simulating N interacting fans in a game engine is at least $O(N)$–$O(N^2)$; each person, flag, chair, ball, and their interactions must be explicitly computed. A World Model instead absorbs "the patterns of the world" into neural-net weights during training, so at inference the entire stadium is a single fixed-cost forward pass regardless of scene complexity. This is what McCormick means by "compute for the uncomputable" — doing non-deterministic computation efficiently.

**2. Actions are the ultimate form of compression.** The load-bearing formal claim: a video model predicts $P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)$ (passive, "a dream you watch"); a World Model predicts $P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t, a_t)$ (interactive, "a lucid dream you shape"). The action $a_t$ carries enough information to unroll future states until the next action updates the picture. Because humans already run this observe→predict→act loop far more efficiently than any engine, harvesting human observation-action data lets you "get all that computation practically for free." Summarized as: **LLMs learn the structure of language; World Models learn the structure of causality.**

**3. Language and code are insufficient.** Two illustrative devices: the "describe clapping your hands by the picosecond" exercise (you can't — language is lossy), and a literary frame from Hesse's *Glass Bead Game* — LLMs are "our Castalians," exquisite symbol-manipulators who "can describe clapping but cannot clap," who "talk about gravity but do not know gravity the way a toddler knows gravity." Real intelligence must come from contact with the world.

**4. World Models help agents three ways:** (a) surrogate training grounds — practice inside the dream, transfer to reality, valuable for safety and sample efficiency; (b) longer-horizon planning — "imagine" consequences before acting; (c) rich representations — an agent learns to see the world in terms of features that matter for acting. The central open question of the field: are simulated environments *faithful enough* that training transfers to reality ("pre-train in sim")? Increasingly the answer is yes (cites Ai2's MolmoBot: sim-only policies beating SOTA VLAs trained on thousands of real hours).

**5. World Model ≠ Policy.** The World Model is the *dream* (takes actions → produces predicted observations); the Policy is the *dreamer* (takes observations → produces actions). Recent research shows the two are intimate: initialize from World Model weights, then fine-tune to predict *actions* rather than *frames* — "understanding and doing are the same skill seen from different angles."

**6. General Intuition's thesis — the data bet.** Every approach hits the same wall: it needs action-labeled data, and raw video lacks action labels ("shadows on Plato's cave wall"). LeCun's answer is to *infer* actions, but inferred actions have "separate scaling laws," cost compute, and fail on edge cases (you can't see a plane's rudder move from the cockpit — "if you don't do it, you crash"). GI's advantage: **Medal**, which collects 1B+ gaming clips/year with recorded in-game *action names* (not keystrokes) alongside exactly what the player saw — "ground truth human data in a digital environment," information-complete (unlike real video, no pose-estimation loss). Analogy: GitHub data trained machines to code; gaming data can train machines to act. GI's strategic move is to "collapse the problem" of transfer curves (below) by standardizing on **game-controller inputs** and **vision-only sensors**.

## Notable details

- **Prehistory / pre-deep-learning (Wave 0, 1990–91):** Schmidhuber's *Making the World Differentiable* (1990, RNN that predicts a simulated world + trains an agent inside it) and Sutton's *Dyna* (1991, unify learning/planning/reacting). In 1990 the world had ~100 trillion–1 quadrillion× less compute; global datasphere ~10 petabytes vs 221 zettabytes by 2026.
- **Ha & Schmidhuber, *World Models* (March 2018):** three components — Vision model **V** (compress pixels), Memory model **M** (RNN predicting next), tiny Controller **C** (the policy). World Model = V+M; agent trained *entirely inside* the hallucinated dream (car-racing + Doom/VizDoom), policy transferred back — and it worked. Baseball analogy: a batter facing a 100mph fastball reacts to the brain's internal-World-Model prediction, not to reality.
- **Four waves:** Wave 1 (2018–19) "Can it work?" — VAE+RNN, SimPLe / Atari 100k benchmark. Wave 2 (2020–22) "Match human performance?" — **DreamerV2** (Hafner, Recurrent State-Space Model with discrete latents, first human-level across 55 Atari games, trained in imagination on a single GPU) vs **MuZero** (plans in *abstract latent* space, never generates anything observable; culmination of AlphaGo→AlphaZero→MuZero where each hard-coded element — rules, strategy, value — is progressively learned). This split births the two schools: **generative** (observable futures) vs **latent** (abstract prediction). Wave 3 (2023–24) "Truly interactive?" — **GAIA-1** (Wayve, 9B params, real driving video, confirmed LLM scaling laws hold for visual World Models); **DIAMOND** (2024, diffusion predicting frames *directly*, richer visual fidelity → better agent performance; produced a playable Counter-Strike neural engine from ~87 hours of footage on one GPU; "the DeepSeek/Llama of generative World Models"). Wave 4 (now) "Can models act in the real world?" — comma.ai (policy trained in a learned World Model, shipped in openpilot — arguably first consumer product from a World-Model-trained agent); Meta **V-JEPA 2** (1.2B params, pre-trained on 1M+ hours self-supervised, fine-tuned on 62 hours Droid robot data, zero-shot planning on real Franka arms in *seconds* vs *minutes* for pixel approaches); DeepMind **SIMA 2** (fine-tune Gemini to act directly).
- **The multimodality / blurriness problem:** naive MSE prediction of a car turning a corner produces a *blurry* average of all outcomes (the implausible middle-of-the-road trajectory). Diffusion commits to a sharp mode; autoregressive-token models stay consistent across sampled tokens; **JEPA sidesteps it entirely** by never decoding to pixels. Distinction: epistemic uncertainty (reducible, e.g. never-seen traffic light) vs aleatoric uncertainty (irreducible randomness, dice).
- **Lab taxonomy (three tiers):** *Current Foundation Models* (not action-conditioned) — LLMs, Video Models (Sora, Veo 3, Kling, Runway...), 3D Reconstruction (World Labs' Marble — persistent 3D scenes but "not what I'd define as a World Model" — not interactive). *World Models proper*, split into **Latent** (LeCun/JEPA — "the world is unpredictable; JEPA is not generative AI"; efficient, better for representation/planning, but hard to evaluate, slow iteration, and prone to representation collapse — the trivial all-zeros encoding gets 0 loss; Moonlake by Manning/Goodfellow/Sun as a hybrid) and **Generative** (GI, Wayve, Decart, Runway GWM-1, Genie 3 — pixel-level, playable, better when visual detail matters; DIAMOND showed richer worlds → smarter agents). *Embodied Agents* — **VLAs** (Physical Intelligence's π₀ / π₀.5 / π*0.6 / MEM; "pragmatist's answer," reuses trillions in LLM infra — framed via Brian Arthur's increasing-returns lock-in and Sara Hooker's "hardware lottery"), **Dreamer/latent agents** (Hafner's new startup Embo, $100M), **SIMA 2** (VLM-backbone generalist).
- **Transfer curves (GI's own framing, "not well understood"):** three curves govern generalization — **input modality transfer** (degrees of freedom; steep for a 20–60-DOF humanoid, easy for a game controller), **sensor transfer** (how much tactile/proprioception/depth data you need — Tesla's LiDAR-dropping problem), **environment transfer** (degradation as scenes get more complex/stochastic/populated). GI "collapses" the first two by betting on game-controller inputs + vision only, leaving environment transfer as the one real question. Claim: "the scaling laws don't exist" for the brute-force "collect tons of data and hope it generalizes" approach.
- **Provocative closing stance:** robots *shouldn't* be humanoid — humanoid form was chosen mainly because internet video is full of humans; if you learn from game-controller actions instead, that assumption collapses, favoring "simpler, cheaper systems with only the degrees of freedom the job needs." Interfaces (joysticks, wheels, gamepads, keyboards) are "decades of iteration compressing human intent into a clean universal action space, much like language does for thought."

## Why it matters [analyst's view]

This is the most useful *map* I've seen of the World Models field circa 2026 — its value is taxonomic and rhetorical, not scientific (it's a vendor essay, and its central "games-as-ground-truth transfers to reality" claim is asserted with "early signs," not demonstrated). The genuinely sharp conceptual contributions: (1) the crisp $P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)$ vs $P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)$ framing that separates World Models from video models — the single cleanest way to explain the distinction; (2) "actions as compression," which reframes the data problem as an *action-label* problem rather than a raw-pixel problem; (3) the three transfer curves as an honest admission that nobody knows the scaling laws for sim-to-real. The essay is in direct tension with Sonia Joseph's [[blogs/joseph-world-models-interpretability]]: McCormick treats the World Model as an *external, generative, playable environment*, whereas Joseph argues the world model is the *internal causal structure of a foundation model* and that generation is beside the point. Reading them together sharpens the field's core definitional split. Also notably one-sided on JEPA — it lists JEPA's practical downsides at length while treating the generative approach's fidelity cost as manageable, which is exactly what you'd expect from a generative-World-Model company.

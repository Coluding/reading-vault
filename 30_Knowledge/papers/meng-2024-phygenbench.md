## TL;DR

PhyGenBench is a benchmark for testing whether text-to-video (T2V) models understand **intuitive physics** — 160 curated prompts covering **27 physical laws** across **4 domains** (Mechanics, Optics, Thermal, Material Properties), each prompt describing a scene with a clear expected physical outcome (e.g. iron placed on water should sink) without revealing that outcome in the text. Because judging physical correctness in video is hard for existing metrics, the paper also proposes **PhyGenEval**, a hierarchical VLM+LLM evaluation pipeline that decomposes physical-commonsense assessment into three stages — key-phenomena detection, physics-order verification, and overall naturalness — combining GPT-4o, VQAScore, LLaVA-Interleave, and InternVideo2. PhyGenEval correlates strongly with human judgment (Spearman ρ = 0.81, Kendall τ = 0.78 on physical commonsense), far above prior metrics like VideoScore (τ=0.17) and VideoPhy (τ=0.03). The headline finding: **even the best model, Gen-3, scores only 0.51** on physical commonsense alignment (PCA), and models are far from being world simulators. Critically, **scaling models and prompt engineering are both insufficient** — scaling helps some static cases but fails on dynamic phenomena, and prompt engineering only fixes trivial issues (e.g. flame color). Models routinely achieve high semantic alignment (~0.80) while violating physics, showing the two are decoupled.

## Context & motivation

T2V models like Sora are increasingly framed as a path toward a universal **world simulator**, but the paper argues their capacity to represent **intuitive physics** is largely unexplored. Current models fail on "seemingly trivial physical phenomenons" — e.g. not understanding that a stone should sink in water. Existing video benchmarks emphasize visual quality, aesthetics, and text-video semantic alignment, but do not test whether the depicted dynamics obey physical laws. A separate difficulty is *evaluation*: general video-quality metrics and VLM-based scorers (VideoScore, DEVIL, VideoPhy) correlate poorly with human judgments of physical correctness. The paper's stated contribution is threefold: (1) **PhyGenBench**, a physics-focused T2V benchmark grounded in explicit physical laws with clear observable outcomes; (2) **PhyGenEval**, an automated hierarchical evaluation framework that matches human feedback closely enough to scale; (3) an **empirical study** showing that even the strongest model (Gen-3, 0.51) is far from a world simulator, and that scaling/prompt-engineering do not close the gap.

## Method

### Problem formulation

Given a text prompt $p$ that describes a scene governed by a known physical law $l$ (with a clear expected physical outcome), and a video $V$ generated by a T2V model from $p$, the task is to score how well $V$ obeys $l$. Evaluation produces two axes, each mapped to a **four-point scale (0–3)**:
- **Semantic Alignment (SA)** — do the specified objects and actions appear?
- **Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA)** — does the video's dynamics obey the physical law?

The benchmark's design intent is that a model can only score well on PCA if it genuinely simulates the physics, not merely renders the objects.

### Core idea

Two coupled contributions: a benchmark of prompts whose *expected physical outcome is deliberately withheld from the text* (so the model must supply physical commonsense itself), plus a **hierarchical, decomposed evaluation** that breaks the hard question "is this physically correct?" into tractable sub-questions answered by different VLMs/LLMs at the single-frame, ordered-frame, and full-video levels.

### Benchmark construction

**27 physical laws across 4 domains** (160 prompts total):

- **Mechanics (7 laws, 40 prompts):** gravity, buoyancy, solid pressure, atmospheric pressure, elasticity, friction, surface tension. Example: "A piece of iron is gently placed on the surface of the water in a tank filled with water" (tests buoyancy — iron should sink).
- **Optics (6 phenomena, 50 prompts):** reflection, refraction, scattering, dispersion, interference & diffraction, straight-line propagation. Example: "A kite soaring above a smooth and tranquil pond" (tests reflection).
- **Thermal (6 phase transitions, 30 prompts):** solidification, melting, liquefaction, boiling, deposition, sublimation. Example: water heated above 100 °C should boil.
- **Material Properties (8 properties, 40 prompts):** physical properties (color, hardness, solubility, combustibility, flame reaction) and chemical properties (acidity, redox potential, dehydrating properties). Example: "An egg being hurled with significant force towards a rock" — egg breaks, rock intact.

**Prompt design — five-step pipeline:**
1. **Conceptualization** — pick physical laws from textbooks that are "easily demonstrated through clear, observable physical phenomenon."
2. **Prompt Engineering** — manually craft an initial prompt depicting the phenomenon.
3. **Prompt Augmentation** — add object/action detail *without revealing the expected outcome*, so the model can't cheat off the text.
4. **Diversity Enhancement** — use GPT-4o for object substitution to diversify.
5. **Quality Control** — manual review to ensure prompt ↔ physical-law correspondence is clear and accurate.

**Statistics:** 160 prompts, 165 unique objects, 42 unique actions, average caption length 18.75 words.

### PhyGenEval — evaluation methodology

PhyGenEval is a hierarchical VLM+LLM pipeline. It computes a **Semantic Alignment (SA)** score and a **Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA)** score from three sub-stages; each is discretized to a 0–3 four-point scale.

**Semantic Alignment (SA).** GPT-4o extracts objects and actions from the prompt, then:
- Q1 (object presence): score 0–2 (2 = all objects present, 1 = some missing, 0 = none).
- Q2 (action execution): binary 0/1 based on whether the action occurs.
VLM used: GPT-4o.

**PCA is computed in three sub-stages:**

**Stage 2A — Key Physical Phenomena Detection (single frame).** A "key physical phenomenon" is an observable, distinctive occurrence that directly reveals the law (deformation, color change, etc.). GPT-4o generates a retrieval prompt $p_r$ and a set of physics questions $Q$; a keyframe (index $i$) is located via $p_r$ and scored:

$$S_{key} = \sum_{q \in Q} \max_{i-2 \le j \le i+2}\big[\text{VLM}(I_j, q) + \text{VLM}(I_j, p_r)\big]$$

where $I_j$ is the $j$-th frame, $\text{VLM}(I_j, q)$ scores presence of the physical phenomenon in frame $I_j$ for question $q$, and $\text{VLM}(I_j, p_r)$ scores whether $I_j$ matches the retrieval prompt. The $\pm 2$-frame tolerance window absorbs keyframe-localization imprecision. Implemented with **VQAScore**.

**Stage 2B — Physics Order Verification (ordered multi-frame).** Ensures key phenomena occur in the correct temporal order, validating causality. GPT-4o generates $p_r$ and three questions: $q_1$ (order correctness from the first frame to the keyframe), $q_2$ (keyframe to last frame), $q_3$ (overall physical-sequence coherence across the full video):

$$S_{before} = \max_{i-2 \le j \le i}\big[\text{VLM}(I_0, I_j, q_1) + \text{VLM}(I_j, p_r)\big]$$
$$S_{after} = \max_{i \le j \le i+2}\big[\text{VLM}(I_j, I_{-1}, q_2) + \text{VLM}(I_j, p_r)\big]$$
$$S_{all} = \text{VLM}(I_0, I_{i-2:i+2}, I_{-1}, q_3)$$
$$S_{order} = S_{before} + S_{after} + S_{all}$$

where $I_0$ is the first frame, $I_{-1}$ the last frame, $i$ the keyframe index. Implemented with GPT-4o and **LLaVA-Interleave** (multi-image comprehension).

**Stage 2C — Overall Naturalness Evaluation (full video).** Naturalness = dynamic progression that matches real-world physical phenomena. Two-stage: (1) establish a general evaluation standard $g_{gen}$ referencing the DEVIL methodology; (2) use prompt $p$, physical law $l$, and $g_{gen}$ to have GPT-4o generate a prompt-specific standard $g_{spec}$. Then:

$$S_{natural} = \text{VLM}(I_{0:-1}, p, l, g_{spec})$$

over the full video $I_{0:-1}$. Implemented with GPT-4o and **InternVideo2**.

**Final score.** $S_{key}$, $S_{order}$, $S_{natural}$ are each discretized to the 0–3 scale, averaged, and floor-rounded to give the PCA score. For robustness, $S_{order}$ is evaluated with both GPT-4o and LLaVA-Interleave, and $S_{natural}$ with both GPT-4o and InternVideo2, with the final score an ensemble of the two.

### Training procedure

_not addressed by the source_ — PhyGenBench/PhyGenEval is a benchmark + evaluation framework; no model is trained. The T2V models evaluated are used off-the-shelf.

### Inference / sampling

Evaluation runs the frozen T2V models to generate videos from the 160 prompts, then applies the PhyGenEval pipeline above. No new generative sampling procedure is introduced.

## Experimental setup

- **Models evaluated (8):** Gen-3, Kling, Vchitect 2.0 (2B), CogVideoX (5B), Open-Sora V1.2 (1.1B), Pika, CogVideoX (2B), Lavie (860M). Mix of proprietary (Gen-3, Kling, Pika) and open-source.
- **Metrics:** Semantic Alignment (SA) and Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA), each 0–3 discretized then normalized; reported per-domain and averaged.
- **Human correlation:** PhyGenEval vs. human ratings via Kendall's τ and Spearman's ρ. Baselines compared: VideoScore, DEVIL, VideoPhy.
- Human evaluation reportedly used a 64-prompt subset of the 160.

## Key results

- **Model ranking (PCA average):** Gen-3 **0.51** > Kling **0.49** > Vchitect 2.0 **0.45** ≈ CogVideoX-5B **0.45** > Open-Sora V1.2 **0.44** ≈ Pika **0.44** > CogVideoX-2B **0.39** > Lavie **0.36**. Even the best model (Gen-3, 0.51) is far from world-simulator competence.
- **Per-domain spread** (approx.): Mechanics 0.30–0.45, Optics 0.43–0.58, Thermal 0.34–0.50, Material 0.32–0.51. Models do best in **Optics** (possible dataset/appearance bias) and worst in **Mechanics** (dynamic, force-driven).
- **Human correlation (PCA):** overall Kendall τ = **0.78**, Spearman ρ = **0.81** (per-domain τ 0.72–0.81); SA correlation τ = 0.53, ρ = 0.56. Vastly better than VideoScore (τ=0.17, ρ=0.19), DEVIL (τ=0.17, ρ=0.18), VideoPhy (τ=0.03, ρ=0.04).
- **Semantic vs. physical decoupling:** models score ~0.80 average on SA but only 0.36–0.51 on PCA — i.e. they render the right objects/actions while violating physics.
- **Scaling is insufficient:** "Scaling up models can solve some issues but still fails to handle dynamic physical phenomenons," which the authors believe requires extensive training on synthetic data.
- **Prompt engineering is insufficient:** prompt engineering (citing Fu et al., 2024) "only solves a few simple issues (e.g., flame color)," not the dynamic ones.

## Ablations

The authors report that the key designs of PhyGenEval are essential, with detailed results in Appendix C.3. The three components — Key Phenomena Detection (2A), Physics Order Verification (2B), and Overall Naturalness Evaluation (2C) — are each shown to contribute to the strong human correlation; removing them degrades agreement. Exact per-ablation numbers are in the appendix and not transcribed here.

## Limitations

- (Author-flagged) Current models broadly struggle to comply with physical commonsense; the gap from world simulation is large.
- (Author-flagged) Better performance in optics suggests a possible domain/appearance bias in what models find easy.
- (Author-flagged) Open-source models substantially underperform proprietary ones.
- [analyst's view, honest-reader flag] Human evaluation used only a **64-prompt subset** of the 160, and the benchmark assumes essentially **one salient physical law per prompt/video** with a single "keyframe," which limits coverage of multi-law or continuously-interacting dynamics. PhyGenEval leans heavily on GPT-4o as both question-generator and judge, so its ceiling is tied to that VLM's physical reasoning.

## Why it matters [analyst's view]

This is the seminal "does video generation obey physics?" benchmark — it reframed the T2V-as-world-simulator claim from an aspiration into something measurable, and gave a concrete, human-correlated number (Gen-3 = 0.51) that later work repeatedly cites as the bar to beat. Its two-part structure (a law-grounded prompt set + a decomposed VLM/LLM evaluator) became a template. Its most durable finding is the **decoupling of semantic alignment from physical correctness** — models can look right and be physically wrong — which motivates the entire subsequent line of "physical-alignment" and "physics world model" research. It also lands a negative result that matters strategically: **neither scale nor prompt engineering closes the physics gap**, pointing toward physics-informed training/data rather than brute-force scaling. Within the vault it sits directly upstream of later benchmarks and critiques (PhyGround, PhyWorld) that push back on its assumptions (limited prompt count, one-law-per-video), and adjacent to the physics-alignment and physics-video-world-model notes.

## Open questions / things to verify

- Exact ablation numbers (Appendix C.3) — how much each of the three PCA sub-stages contributes to the τ/ρ correlation.
- How sensitive PCA scores are to the choice of judge VLM (GPT-4o vs. alternatives) — is the ranking of the 8 models stable?
- Whether the "one keyframe per video" assumption biases against models that depict physics as a gradual continuum rather than a discrete event.
- The claim that synthetic-data training is what's needed for dynamic phenomena is asserted, not demonstrated here — verify against follow-up work.
- Whether the optics advantage is genuine physical understanding or appearance/texture shortcut.
